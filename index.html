01 / 2021 — A new beginning

Canada, Computer Science, and my first restaurant job.

I came to Canada in 2021 to study Computer Science at Lakehead University. Alongside my studies, I started working part-time at a Vietnamese restaurant. While learning about software in class, I was also learning how people, orders, and information move through a restaurant.

02 / Learning through different restaurants

One industry, many ways of working.

I later had opportunities to work in Japanese restaurants, at Chuck’s, and in breakfast service at Pür & Simple. Each environment had its own menu, pace, service expectations, and coordination needs. Those experiences helped me see why restaurant software needs to fit the workflow of the people using it.

03 / Connecting service with data

An order is also a source of questions.

I began to see the orders I handled every day as potential data: what guests choose, which modifications they request, and how information reaches the right station. With the right records and analysis, those details could help us understand recurring needs and opportunities to improve service.

Which modifiers are requested most often? Where do order changes create confusion? What information would help staff coordinate more clearly? These are the questions I want to investigate.

04 / What I’m building now

From observations to small, testable solutions.

With a BSc in Computer Science and hands-on restaurant experience, I’m building an independent Restaurant Tech portfolio. My first prototype explores item-specific modifiers, configurable pricing, and separate savory, sweet, and beverage station tickets.

Next, I want to document service problems, turn them into requirements, build and test possible solutions, and use data to evaluate what helps. I’m developing toward opportunities in business analysis, implementation, and restaurant technology support.

The current project uses fictional café data. The analysis and workflow improvements described here are areas I’m exploring; no live restaurant deployment or measured business impact is claimed.

My approach

Observe a problem. Build a response. Check what changes.

Each project starts with a service question, makes the requirements visible, and separates what the prototype demonstrates from what still needs testing.
